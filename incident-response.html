Incident Response

What to Do After an Incident

Use this page for quick response guidance. Take care of the immediate situation and make the required calls before worrying about documentation.

!

Incident Happening Now?

Take care of the immediate situation first.

Do not delay emergency response, medical attention, or required notifications to use this page or complete documentation.

1

Stop Work & Make the Area Safe

Stop affected work and address immediate hazards only when it can be done safely. Keep others away from the hazard.

2

Get Emergency Help if Needed

For a serious or life-threatening emergency, fire, or other immediate danger, call 911.

3

Notify Your Foreman

Notify your Foreman immediately so the proper response can be coordinated.

4

Notify Superintendent & Safety

Contact your Superintendent and the Safety Department as soon as the immediate situation is being handled.

Additional Guidance

What happened?

Once the immediate situation is being handled, select the incident type below for additional guidance.

Immediate

Protect the injured person and get appropriate help.

  • Stop affected work and make sure the area is safe before approaching or assisting.
  • Call 911 for a serious or life-threatening injury, or whenever emergency medical assistance is needed.
  • Do not move a seriously injured person unless remaining where they are creates a greater immediate danger.
  • Provide first aid only within your level of training and available resources.
  • Notify the Foreman, Superintendent, and Safety Department.
When Safe
  • Preserve the area when practical so the incident can be properly reviewed.
  • Take photos of the area, equipment, conditions, or other relevant details when it is safe to do so.
  • Follow direction from the Superintendent and Safety regarding additional documentation and next steps.
Immediate

Stop work and keep everyone away from the affected area.

  • Stop excavation or other affected work immediately.
  • Keep employees and bystanders away from the damaged utility and surrounding hazard area.
  • Treat damaged electrical lines and anything in contact with them as energized. Do not touch them.
  • If equipment may be in contact with an energized line, stay away from the equipment and do not approach it.
  • Contact the appropriate utility provider to report the damaged line, such as Rocky Mountain Power for an electrical utility strike. Call 911 for a gas release, fire, dangerous electrical condition, injury, or other immediate emergency.
  • Notify the Foreman, Superintendent, and Safety Department immediately.
When Safe
  • Do not attempt to repair, cover, reconnect, or disturb a damaged utility unless specifically authorized and qualified to do so.
  • Take photos of markings, the damaged utility, equipment position, and surrounding conditions only when it is safe.
  • Do not resume affected work until the hazard has been evaluated and you have been directed that work may continue.
Immediate

Check for injuries and protect people from additional danger.

  • Stop and check yourself and others for injuries.
  • Call 911 for every vehicle accident, regardless of severity. Ridge Rock requires police notification and a police report.
  • Stay out of active traffic and do not put yourself in danger while checking vehicles or damage.
  • Notify the Foreman, Superintendent, and Safety Department.
When Safe
  • Take photos of all involved vehicles, visible damage, the surrounding area, roadway, and relevant conditions.
  • Gather the information needed from other involved drivers or parties when appropriate.
  • Do not argue about fault or make unnecessary statements about who caused the accident.
  • Follow direction from the Superintendent and Safety regarding towing, vehicle use, documentation, and additional reporting.
Immediate

Stop work if continued operation could create another hazard.

  • Check for injuries and any immediate hazards created by the damage.
  • Shut down or isolate damaged equipment when it can be done safely.
  • Keep employees away from unstable, damaged, or potentially hazardous equipment or property.
  • Notify the Foreman, Superintendent, and Safety Department.
When Safe
  • Take photos of the damage, equipment, surrounding area, and relevant conditions.
  • Do not operate damaged equipment until it has been evaluated and cleared for use.
  • Preserve damaged components or other relevant evidence when practical.
  • Follow direction from the Superintendent and Safety regarding repairs, documentation, and next steps.
Immediate

Protect people first and prevent the situation from getting worse.

  • Stop work. Do not approach, touch, or attempt to identify an unknown material by smelling it.
  • For hazardous or unknown materials, keep everyone away from the affected area and call 911 immediately.
  • Do not investigate, contain, or clean up hazardous or unknown releases. Stay clear and wait for emergency responders and Safety direction.
  • For known, low-risk spills, trained and authorized personnel may use absorbents or sand to protect storm drains or waterways, only if safe to do so.
  • Do not approach large, uncontrolled, flammable, or otherwise dangerous spills. Call 911 immediately if there is an emergency.
  • Notify the Foreman, Superintendent, and Safety Department.
When Safe
  • Identify the material only if already known, and consult the Safety Data Sheet when appropriate. Do not approach or investigate an unknown release.
  • Take photos of the affected area, source, material, and surrounding conditions.
  • Do not dispose of contaminated material or resume affected work until directed by the Superintendent or Safety.

Remember

Safety and communication come first.

Take care of immediate hazards and injuries first. Notify the Superintendent and Safety promptly. Documentation and photos can be handled once the situation is under control and it is safe to do so.
